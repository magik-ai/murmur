**What happened (2026-02-26, drafting his conference talk):** I wrote the on-screen lines
in the punchy register that decks tend to use, all short sentences and full stops for
effect. He read two of them, said he does not speak that way, and asked for the whole
thing rewritten without the theatrical pauses.

**Why it matters:** he delivers these lines himself. His real speech runs long and
slightly rambling, joined by "and", "so", "because" and "at some point". A clipped
aphorism is unreadable in his mouth and lands, to him, as something a machine wrote.

**How to apply:** for anything he will say aloud or publish under his name, write one
flowing sentence per idea, keep the connecting words, stay in the first person, and avoid
slogans. Where he has already said a thing in an interview or a message, reuse his exact
phrasing instead of improving it. The same register applies to release notes and product
copy that carries his voice.
